How the decoder works

Mandarin has a small set of syllables, so many words share the same sound. Number slang (数字谐音, shùzì xiéyīn) swaps words for digits that sound alike. Our decoder first looks for a known code, then splits your number into the longest known pieces (like 9999 + 20), and finally reads any leftover digits by their most common homophone.

FAQ

Why do Chinese people use numbers as words?

Numbers are fast to type on phones and pagers, and many digits sound like common words. Codes like 520 (I love you) and 88 (bye-bye) became mainstream internet slang in the late 1990s and 2000s.

What does 5201314 mean?

5201314 reads as 我爱你一生一世 — “I love you for a lifetime.” It combines 520 (I love you) and 1314 (a whole lifetime).

Which numbers should I avoid in a love message?

Avoid 4 (sounds like “death”), 250 (an insult meaning “fool”) and 748 (“go away”). Prefer 520, 1314, 9 and 8.

520 Day: China's “I Love You” Day

Quick answer: May 20 is “520”, and 五二零 (wǔ èr líng) sounds like 我爱你 (wǒ ài nǐ) — “I love you.” It has become one of China's biggest days for confessions, gifts, proposals and weddings.

Where 520 came from

Number slang spread in the pager and early-internet era, when typing digits was faster than characters. Social media and e-commerce platforms turned it into an annual shopping moment.

A marriage boom on a single day

Couples compete for marriage-registration appointments on May 20. In 2026, all time slots across Hangzhou's main urban areas and all 15 registration offices in Guangzhou's 11 districts reached zero remaining quota, while Chengdu saw 2,500+ of 3,100+ slots reserved. China registered 6.763 million marriages in 2025, up 10.76% year-on-year.

What people buy

Flowers, jewellery, perfume, cosmetics, electronics and experiences. In one 520 period, Douyin reported “520” searches and purchases up 1.4× year-on-year, with perfume sales +186% and lipstick +143%. Many couples also send red envelopes of 520 or 1314 yuan.

And 521?

May 21 (521) reads as 我愿意, “I'm willing / I do” — so the two days work as a pair: one asks, one answers.

FAQ

Is 520 a public holiday in China?

No. It is an unofficial, internet-born love day, but it is widely celebrated and heavily promoted by retailers.

What should I say on 520?

Simply “520” or “我爱你”. For more, try 5201314 — “I love you for a lifetime.”

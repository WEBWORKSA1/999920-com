Chinese Wedding Traditions & Their Lucky Numbers

From the date to the dowry, numbers shape a Chinese wedding. Here's what each custom means and how modern couples adapt it.

1. Choosing an auspicious date

Families traditionally consult the almanac (黄历) and both partners' birth details. Modern couples also favour number-rich dates: May 20 (520), September 9 (久久), and even-numbered “paired” dates, while avoiding 4s. Try the Wedding Date Finder.

2. Betrothal gifts (过大礼)

The groom's family sends gifts to the bride's family — often in even numbers and pairs, symbolising a couple. Common items include wine, tea, sweets, fruit, dragon-and-phoenix candles and a bride price in a red envelope, frequently in lucky amounts (e.g. containing 8s and 9s).

3. Wedding gold

Gold jewellery — especially 999.9 (“four nines”) 24K gold — is a blessing and a store of value. Dragon-and-phoenix bangles (龙凤镯) are a signature piece.

4. The tea ceremony (敬茶)

The couple kneel or bow and serve tea to parents and elders, who in return give red envelopes or gold jewellery. It formally welcomes the new spouse into the family.

6. The banquet

Multi-course banquets often feature dishes whose names sound auspicious — fish (鱼 ≈ 余, abundance), and sweet soup for a sweet marriage. The 囍 “double happiness” character decorates everything.

FAQ

How much should I give in a Chinese wedding red envelope?

It depends on your relationship and local norms; choose an even amount, avoid 4s, and consider lucky combinations with 8, 9, 520 or 1314.

What does the double happiness symbol mean?

囍 (shuāng xǐ) joins two 喜 (joy) characters: joy for both partners and families.

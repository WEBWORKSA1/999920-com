Qixi: Chinese Valentine's Day

Quick answer: Qixi (七夕, “Night of Sevens”) falls on the 7th day of the 7th lunar month — August 8, 2027 and August 26, 2028.

The legend

Zhinü, the Weaver Girl, a celestial weaver, fell in love with Niulang, a mortal cowherd. Heaven separated them with the Silver River — the Milky Way. Once a year, on the seventh night of the seventh month, magpies form a bridge so the lovers can meet. The stars Vega and Altair represent the pair.

Traditions

  • Young women traditionally prayed to the Weaver Girl for skill and a good match (乞巧, “begging for skills”).
  • Stargazing for Vega and Altair.
  • Today: dates, flowers, jewellery, proposals and weddings.

Qixi vs Valentine's Day

Valentine's Day is fixed on February 14; Qixi moves with the lunar calendar. Valentine's is rooted in a Christian saint's day; Qixi in a Chinese folk legend about separated lovers — which is why long-distance couples especially love it.

Is Qixi the same as Tanabata?

Japan's Tanabata comes from the same legend but is usually celebrated on July 7 in the Gregorian calendar.
